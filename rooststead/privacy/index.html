Privacy Policy

Effective November 23, 2026

Rooststead (on the App Store, Rooststead: Field Vet) is a home animal-care manual for iPhone and iPad. It is made and run by Angel Tech Corporation Industries L.L.C., an Oklahoma limited liability company, 811 N Market, Watonga, Oklahoma 73772, USA (“we”, “us”). This policy says what happens to your information when you use the app.

The short version
  • No account, no ads and no tracking.
  • Everything you save (your animals, the lump log, the library, your lists and settings) stays on your device.
  • A lookup sends the animal’s details, what you typed and any photos you attached (and, for a Field Computer question, that conversation and the animal’s lump log) to our server, which passes them to Anthropic’s Claude to write the answer. None of that is sent until you agree in the app.
  • Our server does not keep what you typed, your photos or the answers.
  • To make the answers better, it keeps counts of what is looked up (the kind of lookup, the species, the subject in a few words, and the country or US state). The counts are tied to no one, and you can turn them off in Settings.
  • “Did this help?” under an answer sends your yes or no. It sends your note, or the question and answer, only if you choose.
  • Rooststead Pro is bought through Apple. We never see your payment details.
  • We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for advertising.

What stays on your device

The app saves these on your device, inside the app:

  • your animals on file and what you entered about each one;
  • the lump log;
  • your library of filed answers;
  • the medication and grass lists you have looked up, and the region you typed for the grass guide;
  • your settings, including whether you have agreed to the lookup notice.

There is no account and nothing syncs. We cannot see these records. They leave your device only as part of a lookup you ask for (the next section), or when you make a backup and send it somewhere yourself.

Backup and restore makes a copy of your records for you to keep. It goes only where you put it. If you back up your iPhone or iPad with iCloud or a computer, that backup may include the app’s records, under your own Apple settings.

What a lookup sends

A lookup is a question the app asks our server for you: a diagnostic, a care protocol, a research question, the medication reference, a medication lookup, the grass guide, a grass lookup or a question to the Field Computer that it cannot answer on your device. Before your first lookup the app shows what will be sent and to whom, and asks you to agree. Nothing about your animals, nothing you typed and no photo is sent before you agree.

Each lookup sends:

  • The animal’s details for that lookup: species, breed, age, weight, whether it is spayed or neutered, allergies and your notes about it.
  • What you typed or picked for that lookup, such as the areas affected, how long it has been going on, your description, your question, a product or plant name, and a region if you typed one.
  • Any photos you attached (up to three, on a diagnostic lookup or a photo comparison). Photos you choose to donate are described under “Photos you donate” below.
  • For a Field Computer question: your question, the earlier lines of that conversation (eight at most) and, when you have chosen an animal for it, that animal’s lump log entries (where, size, feel, note and date). In lines the Field Computer wrote from your records, an animal’s name is replaced before sending. What you typed yourself, or a suggested question you tapped, is sent as it reads.
  • The app’s random device id. The app makes this id itself the first time it runs. It is not your device’s serial number or an advertising id, and it is not linked to your name, your Apple Account or any other app. Its only job is to let the server count the free lookups and keep to the limits described below. It is kept in your device’s secure storage (the Keychain), so it can stay the same if you delete the app and install it again.
  • Apple’s signed record of your subscription, when Rooststead Pro is active, so the server can check that it is real. It names the plan, its dates and price, the App Store country, Apple’s transaction numbers and a few technical fields Apple adds. It does not contain your name, your email, your Apple Account or your payment details.

When an answer comes with reference photos, the app asks our server to find them. That request sends the names of the conditions or plants and the species, with the same device id and subscription record.

To show your plan and how many lookups you have left, the app may also ask our server with just the device id and, for subscribers, the subscription record. That check carries nothing about you or your animals.

The app uses the camera or your photos only when you choose to add a photo to a lookup, and it uses only the photos you take or pick. It does not ask for your location.

Who receives it

  • Our server. It runs on Cloudflare, our hosting provider.
  • Anthropic. Our server passes the lookup (the animal’s details, what you typed and any photos) to Anthropic’s Claude models, which write the answer. The device id and Apple’s subscription record are not passed to Anthropic.

No one else receives your lookups. We do not sell them, and no advertiser sees them.

What our server keeps

The server works on a lookup only while it is answering it. Once the answer has been sent back to the app, the server keeps none of what you typed, none of your photos and none of the answer. The one exception is feedback you choose to send: see “Did this help?” below.

It keeps these counts so the limits work:

  • Free lookups and free photo requests used by each install. The counts are kept under a scrambled form of the device id. The scrambling works one way: the code cannot be turned back into the id. These counts are kept so the free lookups cannot be reset.
  • Today’s lookups and photo requests for each subscription, under a scrambled form of Apple’s subscription number. They are deleted shortly after the day ends.
  • Today’s free lookups from each internet connection, under a scrambled form of the network’s address, and a total for the whole app. These guard against abuse and are deleted shortly after the day ends. Short per-minute limits work the same way.
  • How many “Did this help?” answers each install sent today, under the same scrambled form of the device id, so feedback cannot be flooded. Deleted shortly after the day ends.
  • One log line for each request: the kind of lookup, whether the plan was free or Pro, whether it worked and how long it took.

It does not keep or log:

  • what you typed, your animals’ details, your photos or the answers, except feedback you choose to send;
  • your name, email or Apple Account, which it never receives;
  • the device id itself, or Apple’s transaction numbers;
  • your internet (IP) address. Like any internet service, our server receives that address when the app connects. It uses it only to make the scrambled network code above;
  • your location more exactly than a country or a US state (see below), or any advertising id.

What we keep to make the answers better

Rooststead’s answers are written by AI. To learn what people need, what helps and what is wrong, the server keeps tallies of what is looked up. For each answered lookup it adds one to these counts:

  • The kind of lookup (for example a diagnostic or a medication lookup), whether the plan was free or Pro, and whether the answer was marked urgent.
  • The species.
  • The subject, in a few plain words, such as “malassezia dermatitis”, “terbinafine” or “johnson grass”. It is the name of the condition, product or plant the lookup was about, or a short general subject the AI names for a question. It is never the sentence you typed, and anything that looks like a name, a number, an address or an email is left out.
  • The country, or the US state, the request came from. Our server reads this from a note its host (Cloudflare) puts on each request. It is worked out from your internet address. The address itself is not kept.

These are counts by day and by week. They are not tied to your install, your subscription or your internet address, and they are deleted after 400 days. We also count how many lookups failed and how many were stopped because the free lookups were used up.

You can turn the tallies off. In the app open Settings, tap Manage under Privacy & data, and tap Disable under Usage analytics. The server then keeps no tallies for your lookups, and “Did this help?” is not shown.

“Did this help?”

Under an answer the app asks “Did this help?”. Nothing is sent unless you tap Yes or No. Your answer is then sent, with the app’s device id (used only for the daily limit above), and counted against that answer’s subject. If you tap No you can pick a reason and add a short note, and you can tick a box to send the question and the answer as text so we can check it. Photos are never sent with feedback, and the app does not include your animal’s name.

After a yes or no, the app asks What are you doing about it? (home care, a veterinarian, a store or pharmacy product, or not sure yet). If you tap one, it is sent with the same ticket and only counted against that answer’s subject, like the yes or no; nothing else about it is kept.

We keep feedback for up to 400 days, at most the newest 5,000, and it is not tied to your install. We read it to correct and improve the answers. Please do not put personal details in a note.

What others see

Under an answer the app can show Others with this: totals from everyone who looked up the same subject in the last four weeks, namely how many lookups, the share of those who answered “Did this help?” who said yes, and the share who chose home care, a veterinarian or a store or pharmacy product. They are made only from the counts above, never from one person’s lookup, and each total is shown only once at least 10 lookups or answers are behind it. They come back with the answer to your own lookup, so nothing extra is sent to get them.

Anthropic and Cloudflare

Anthropic and Cloudflare handle this data for us, as our service providers, under their own terms. Each may keep request data for a limited time for safety and to prevent abuse. Their own pages say how: Anthropic’s privacy policy and Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

Our server and our providers operate in the United States and other countries, so a lookup may be handled outside the country you are in.

Photos you donate

After a diagnostic lookup with a photo, the app offers Donate this photo to teach Rooststead’s photo model. Nothing is donated unless you tap it, say what the problem turned out to be (or that it is not known yet), and tick that you took the photos or may share them and that they show no people. It is off while Help improve Rooststead or AI lookups is turned off.

  • What is sent and kept: the photos of that lookup (as the app redrew them, which leaves out the camera details and any location stored in the original), the species, the affected areas, what it turned out to be, who said so (a veterinarian, your own judgment, or not confirmed), and the AI’s best match as a hint. Not your animal’s name, not your notes, not your device id, not your address.
  • What for: to train and test Rooststead’s own photo recognition and to check Rooststead’s answers. Never sold, never shown to other people as photos, never used for advertising.
  • How long: until you delete it, and at most 3 years.
  • Changing or deleting it: the app keeps a receipt for each donation on your device. In Settings, under Privacy & data, Contributed images, you can say what it turned out to be, or delete one or all of them from our server. Erase everything on this device deletes them from our server first. If you deleted the app before deleting a donation, email us the date and what it showed and we will find and delete it. A model already trained with a photo keeps what it learned from it.
  • The app sends its device id with a donation only to limit donations to 10 a day per install; that count is deleted after the day.

Reference photos

Some answers show reference photos from Wikimedia Commons. Our server finds them by searching Wikimedia Commons for the name of the condition or plant, with the species. Anthropic’s Claude then picks which of the photos found, if any, fits the name. That search comes from our server, not from your device, and carries nothing about you.

Photo comparison. After a diagnostic lookup with a photo, you can tap Compare my photo with the reference photos. It is a lookup: the app sends your photos again, with the addresses of the reference photos it showed you. Our server fetches those reference photos from Wikimedia Commons (Wikimedia sees our server, not you) and sends them with yours to Anthropic’s Claude, which says which looks closest. Nothing of it is kept on our server.

When a photo is shown, your device loads it straight from Wikimedia’s servers. So Wikimedia sees your device’s internet (IP) address and the usual technical details of a web request, as any website does when you load a picture from it. Wikimedia’s privacy policy applies to that.

Rooststead Pro purchases

Rooststead Pro is sold through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles the payment and your Apple Account under Apple’s privacy policy. We never receive your name, your payment details or your Apple Account. The app asks Apple, on your device, whether Rooststead Pro is active, and sends Apple’s signed record with a lookup so our server can check it. Apple also gives us sales and subscription reports that do not identify you.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell your personal information.
  • We do not share it for advertising.
  • There are no ads in the app.
  • There is no tracking across apps or websites.
  • There is no advertising software and no outside analytics software (SDK) in the app. The tallies described above are kept by our own server.

Children

Rooststead is made for adults who look after animals. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, email us at the address below.

Erasing your information

  • On your device: open Settings in the app and tap Erase everything on this device. Deleting the app also deletes what it saved. The random device id is not one of your records. It may stay in your device’s Keychain, including after the app is deleted.
  • On our server: donated photos are deleted from Settings (see “Photos you donate”). The counts described above are kept under scrambled codes that we cannot match to a person. Feedback you chose to send (your note, and the question and answer if you ticked the box) is kept as text for up to 400 days and is not tied to your install. If you tell us what it said, we can find it and delete it. Email us and we will answer within 45 days.
  • With our providers: anything Anthropic or Cloudflare kept for safety is deleted on their own schedules, under their policies linked above.

Withdrawing the AI permission

You can take back your agreement to the lookup notice at any time: in the app open Settings, tap Manage under Privacy & data, and tap Revoke under Data processing consent. After that the app sends no lookups until you agree again. Everything that works without a lookup keeps working. Withdrawing does not undo lookups that were already sent.

You can also turn AI lookups off: open Settings in the app, find AI lookups and tap Turn off. Then the app sends no lookups at all, and its answers come from a guide written into the app and stored on your device. The same guide answers when a lookup cannot be made, for example with no connection; nothing is sent for those answers.

Security

Lookups travel to our server over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). The server keeps no lookup content, except feedback text people choose to send, so nothing else of a lookup is stored there to be exposed. The records on your device are protected by your device’s own passcode and security: anyone who can unlock your device can open the app and read them. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise that nothing will ever go wrong.

Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, for example in a US state with a consumer privacy law, you may have the right to know what personal information a business holds about you, to get a copy of it, to correct it, to delete it, and to opt out of its sale, of sharing for targeted advertising and of certain profiling (we do none of those three). Our server keeps counts under scrambled codes and the feedback text people choose to send, which is not tied to an install. We can find, copy or delete feedback of yours only if you tell us what it said; for everything else we normally have nothing we can match to a particular person. You can still send a request to angeltechcorporationindustries@gmail.com. We answer within 45 days and tell you what we found. If we decline a request we explain why, and you can appeal by replying to that message. We never treat anyone differently for using these rights. If you live in the European Union, the United Kingdom or another place with a similar law, the same applies: a lookup is sent only with your agreement, which you can withdraw at any time, and you can also complain to your local data protection authority.
