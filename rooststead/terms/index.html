Read this section first

Rooststead gives animal-care reference information. That is all it gives.

  • It is written by AI and can be wrong. Lookups are answered by an AI system (Anthropic’s Claude) from what you enter. No veterinarian reads your lookup or checks the answer. An answer can be wrong, incomplete or out of date, and it can miss what only a hands-on exam or a laboratory test would find.   • It is not veterinary advice, a diagnosis or a treatment. Rooststead is not a veterinarian or a veterinary practice, and we do not practice veterinary medicine.   • No veterinarian-client-patient relationship. Using Rooststead does not create one, with us or with anyone.
  • Confirm every dose and drug. Doses, products and drug names are published reference figures. Before you give an animal anything, confirm the drug, the dose and whether it is safe for that species, age, weight and condition with the product label and a veterinarian. A drug that is safe for one species can kill another.
  • Prescription (Rx) drugs need a veterinarian. Never give an animal a prescription drug, or use any drug in a way its label does not allow, without a veterinarian’s order.
  • Animals that produce food. For animals kept for meat, milk or eggs, the law limits which drugs may be used and sets withdrawal times before the food may be used or sold.   • Diseases that must be reported, and diseases that spread to people. Some animal diseases (for example avian influenza or rabies) must be reported to the authorities, and some can pass to people. Follow your state veterinarian, your public health office and your doctor. Rooststead does not report anything for you.
  • Emergencies. In an emergency, contact a veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic at once. Do not wait for a lookup, and do not rely on one. The Tow-it-in list and the emergency flags are not complete: an answer that flags no emergency does not mean there is none.
  • Not for people. Rooststead is for animals only. Never use it for a person. If a person may be hurt, sick or poisoned, call your local emergency number or a doctor.
  • Professional users.
