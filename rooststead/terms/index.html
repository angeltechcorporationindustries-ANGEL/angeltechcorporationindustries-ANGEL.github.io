1. What Rooststead is

Rooststead is a home animal-care manual for dogs, cats, poultry and livestock. It has two parts:

  • Your records, which stay on your device: animals on file, the lump log, the Tow-it-in list, the library, backup and restore, and settings.
  • Lookups, which our server answers: diagnostics, care protocols, research, the medication reference and medication lookups, the grass guide and grass lookups. Some answers come with reference photos.

The screens call the manual “Field Vet”. That is the manual’s title. It does not mean that a veterinarian is involved.

2. Reference information, not a veterinarian

Read this section first

Rooststead gives animal-care reference information. That is all it gives.

  • It is written by AI and can be wrong. Answers are written by an AI system (Anthropic’s Claude) from what you enter. No veterinarian reads your lookup or checks the answer. An answer can be wrong, incomplete or out of date, and it can miss what only a hands-on exam or a laboratory test would find.
  • It is not veterinary advice, a diagnosis or a treatment. Rooststead is not a veterinarian or a veterinary practice. A list of conditions that may fit, a match score or a reference photo is not a diagnosis. No government agency, veterinary board or other authority has reviewed or approved Rooststead or its answers.
  • No veterinarian-client-patient relationship. Using Rooststead does not create one, with us or with anyone.
  • Confirm every dose and drug. Doses, products and drug names are published reference figures. Before you give an animal anything, confirm the drug, the dose and whether it is safe for that species, age, weight and condition with the product label and a veterinarian. A drug that is safe for one species can kill another.
  • Prescription (Rx) drugs need a veterinarian. Never give an animal a prescription drug without a veterinarian’s prescription.
  • Animals that produce food. For animals kept for meat, milk or eggs, there are legal rules on which drugs may be used and how long to wait before the food is used. Check them with a veterinarian.
  • Emergencies. In an emergency, contact a veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic at once. Do not wait for a lookup, and do not rely on one. The Tow-it-in list and the emergency flags are not complete: an answer that flags no emergency does not mean there is none.
  • Not for people. Rooststead is for animals only. Never use it for a person. If a person may be hurt, sick or poisoned, call your local emergency number or a doctor.
  • You are responsible for your animals’ care. You decide what to do for your animals. Use Rooststead to get ready to talk with your veterinarian, not to replace one.
