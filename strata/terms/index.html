A reference, not professional advice

Strata’s descriptions, keys, tables and calculators are for learning and for first identifications in the field. Minerals and rocks vary, many look alike, and a confident answer from a hand specimen can still be wrong: a laboratory test is the only sure identification. The clinometer and compass use your phone’s sensors, which are less accurate than a real compass-clinometer and can be thrown off by nearby steel. Ages on the time scale follow the International Chronostratigraphic Chart and change as the science does.

Don’t use Strata as the basis for engineering, construction, surveying, mining, mineral-rights, legal, medical or other safety-critical decisions. Check anything that matters with a qualified professional.

Field safety

Field geology involves hammers, sharp rock chips, acid, cliffs, quarries, road cuts, weather and remote places. These hazards can cause serious injury or death.

  • Wear eye protection when anyone near you is hammering. Never hammer at a face above your head.
  • Keep away from loose cliffs, quarry faces and road cuts with traffic. Tell someone where you are going.
  • Handle acid with care and keep it labeled. Some minerals are toxic, radioactive or fibrous (for example cinnabar, arsenic minerals, uranium minerals and asbestos in serpentinite): don’t lick, crush or inhale them, and wash your hands.
  • Collect only where it is allowed. Many parks, monuments and private lands forbid collecting; follow the laws where you are.
