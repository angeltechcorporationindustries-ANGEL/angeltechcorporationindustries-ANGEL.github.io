What it is

Blackletter is bar exam practice that checks its own work. Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, writes fresh practice questions in the format of the NextGen bar exam or the legacy Uniform Bar Exam, and a second, separate model solves each one again before you see it. If the two disagree, the question is thrown out and not counted.

A study aid, not legal advice

Blackletter helps you practice for the bar exam. It is not legal advice and not a bar review course, and it cannot promise that you will pass. Questions, explanations and grades written by AI can be wrong.

  • Check what matters against your bar review course and the official study materials.
  • Its questions are written fresh. They are not real exam questions.
  • Blackletter is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Conference of Bar Examiners (NCBE) or any bar admission authority.

The Terms of Use say this in full.

Practice questions, checked twice

  • Your choice of set: a subject, your weak spots or a mix, one answer of four or, as on the NextGen exam, two of six.
  • After each one: the rule, why it applies, why each wrong answer fails, and where to check the rule.
  • Ask about this question: a follow-up answer about any question.
  • Throw out this question: if one still looks wrong, it leaves your scores and your missed list.

The rules, on spaced review

  • 144 black-letter rules across Civil Procedure, Constitutional Law, Contracts, Criminal Law and Procedure, Evidence, Real Property, Torts, Business Associations and Family Law. They work with no connection.
  • Spaced review: rules you know come back less often; rules you miss come back sooner.
  • Your own rules, and six new cards from Claude on any topic, checked for errors first.

Timed writing, graded line by line

  • Short-answer sets in the NextGen style, essays, and performance tasks with an assigning memo, a File and a Library.
  • Written against the clock on ruled legal-pad pages.
  • A grade with what you hit, what you missed and the changes that would raise the score.

Your day on one page

A daily checklist, your streak, accuracy by subject and a countdown to your exam date.
