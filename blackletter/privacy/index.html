Privacy Policy

Version 2026-11-23 · Effective November 23, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information Blackletter (on the App Store, Blackletter: Bar Exam Practice), the bar exam study app for iPhone and iPad, and these web pages use, why, and the choices you have. Blackletter is made by Angel Tech Corporation Industries L.L.C., an Oklahoma limited liability company, 811 N Market, Watonga, Oklahoma 73772, USA (“we”, “us”, “our”). This policy is part of the Terms of Use (EULA).

The short version
  • No account, no sign-in, no ads and no tracking. We never sell your personal information or share it for advertising.
  • Your study records stay on your device.
  • The AI features send only what they need: the subject and topic you choose, your written answers for grading and your follow-up questions. Nothing is sent before you agree to the AI notice in the app, and you can withdraw in Settings.
  • Our server passes those requests to Anthropic’s Claude and keeps no copy of them or of the answers, only counts under scrambled codes.
  • Apple handles every payment. We never see your name, email or card.

What stays on your device

Everything you do in Blackletter is saved in the app’s own storage on your device: your exam and exam date, the daily checklist and streak, your answers and scores, your missed questions, the rules and their review schedule, your own rules, your written answers and their grades, timers, settings, and the dates you agreed to the Terms and to the AI notice. None of it is sent to us except as part of an AI request you make (below). Deleting the app deletes it. We cannot see it, back it up or restore it for you.

What the AI features send

Practice questions, follow-up answers, rule cards, writing tasks and grading are answered by our server with the help of Anthropic’s Claude, an AI model. Before the first one, the app shows what will be sent and to whom and asks you to agree. Nothing is sent before you tap I agree. Each request sends only what it needs:

  • Practice questions: the exam format (NextGen or the legacy UBE), whether you asked for harder questions, the subjects and topics chosen (up to five at a time), and the opening words of up to 12 questions you saw recently, so that new ones do not repeat them.
  • A writing task: the kind (essay, short-answer set or performance task), the subject and up to three topics.
  • Grading: the task, as our server wrote it, and your written answer.
  • A follow-up answer: the question you are on, the answers you picked, the earlier follow-ups about that question, and what you type.
  • Rule cards: the subject and the topic.

With every request the app also sends a random install number that it made itself and keeps in your device’s Keychain (it is not your name, your Apple Account or an advertising identifier; it counts your free tries) and, if you subscribe, Apple’s signed record of your subscription (it shows the product, the dates and Apple’s transaction numbers, not your name, email or payment details). Your device’s internet (IP) address reaches our server as part of any connection.

Please do not put personal or confidential information, about yourself or anyone else, in your answers or questions. Write about the practice material only.

Who receives it

Our server, which runs on Cloudflare, receives each request and passes what is needed to write the answer to Anthropic. Nobody else receives it.

What our server keeps

Our server keeps no copy of what you send or of the answers. It keeps only these counts, to give out the free tries and the daily allowances fairly and to stop abuse:

  • For each install, how many free tries of each kind it has used. The count is kept under a scrambled code made from the install number with a secret that only our server knows (a one-way SHA-256 hash), never the install number itself. These counts are kept for as long as the service runs, so that free tries do not come back after reinstalling.
  • For each subscription, that day’s counts, under a scrambled code made from Apple’s transaction number.
  • For each internet connection, that day’s count of free requests, under a scrambled code made from the internet address.
  • The day’s total for the whole app.

Daily counts are erased after the day ends (midnight UTC). Cloudflare also counts requests from each install and each internet connection for one minute at a time, to slow down floods. Our server checks Apple’s signed record of a subscription each time and does not keep it. When something goes wrong, our server writes a short error note, such as “request failed: timeout”, with nothing you sent in it; Cloudflare keeps those notes for a few days.

Statistics that identify no one

We may use totals made from these counts, such as how many practice questions were written in a day, to run and plan Blackletter. They contain no individual record and nothing anyone typed. We publish or share no total made from fewer than 10 people, and we never try to identify anyone from a total.

Anthropic and Cloudflare

Anthropic and Cloudflare handle this data for us, as our service providers. Their terms with us let them use it only to provide and secure their services to us, not for advertising, and Anthropic’s commercial terms say it does not train its models on it. Each may keep request data for a limited time for safety and to prevent abuse. Their own pages say how: Anthropic’s privacy policy and Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

Our server and our providers operate in the United States and other countries, so a request may be handled outside the country you are in.

Blackletter Pro purchases

Apple sells Blackletter Pro and takes the payment. We never receive your name, email address, Apple Account or payment details. The app asks Apple whether you subscribe, and our server checks Apple’s signed record to give Pro its daily allowances. Apple’s own privacy policy covers how Apple handles purchases.

What we do not do

No account. No ads. No tracking across apps or websites, and no advertising identifier. No analytics or crash-reporting kits from other companies. We do not ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or health data. We do not sell personal information and do not share it for advertising.

Children

Blackletter is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us information, email us and we will erase what we can find.

Erasing your information

Deleting the app erases everything it saved on your device. Our server holds no content you sent, and its counts are kept only under scrambled codes that we cannot link to you, so there is nothing on it that we could find for you or give back. The count of free tries for an install stays, so that free tries do not come back after a reinstall; it says only how many were used. If you email us, we will answer any question about this.

Withdrawing the AI agreement

In the app, open Settings and tap Withdraw my agreement. From then on nothing is sent, and the app asks again before the next AI request. The built-in rules, your own rules, spaced review, your missed questions and your scores keep working without it.

Security

Every request travels encrypted (HTTPS). Our server signs each practice question and task it writes, so that they cannot be forged or changed, and its secrets are kept in Cloudflare’s encrypted settings. No system is perfectly secure, but because our server keeps no content, a break-in would find none of your answers or questions.

Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live (for example California and other US states), you may have the right to know what personal information we have about you, to get a copy, to correct it, to delete it, and to opt out of its sale or of its sharing for targeted advertising. We do not sell or share personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of. To use any right, email us. We may ask a question to confirm that the request is yours, and because we keep no account and no content we may hold nothing we can tie to you. You may use an authorized agent. We will not treat you differently for using your rights. We answer within 45 days, and if we refuse a request you can appeal by replying to our answer.
