3. A game, not advice about real animals

The game’s animals behave much as real ones do, and the field journal’s facts are written to be true, but the game simplifies nature and can be wrong. Do not use it to decide how to act around real wildlife or in the outdoors. Real moose are large and can be dangerous, especially cows with calves and bulls in the autumn rut, and wolves and other wild animals can be too: keep your distance, never feed or approach them, and follow the advice of local wildlife authorities.

Take breaks. If you feel dizzy, sick, or have pain or discomfort while playing, stop. A very small number of people can have seizures when they see certain flashing lights or patterns, even with no history of them: if you or a family member has had seizures or epilepsy, ask a doctor before playing.
